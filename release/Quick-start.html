HASTUR RETOUCH · 0.1.0-beta.1

Your portrait workspace

Import a portrait, make local corrections, and export a separate finished image. Your original file stays intact. AI runs on your computer.

This is a beta. Check face details at 100% before sharing exports and keep your originals. The first AI analysis and the first stroke after changing the view can take longer.

Start editing

  1. Install the beta, then open Hastur Retouch Beta from Start. Models and runtimes are included; internet, Rust and Python are not needed for editing.
  2. Choose Import, drag a photo into the workspace, or press Ctrl+O. JPEG, PNG, TIFF, WebP and BMP are supported. RAW files are not supported.
  3. Use Auto Retouch for facial cleanup, then adjust the individual strengths. Auto Retouch preserves your existing contrast, exposure and shape settings. Face reshaping and background changes are explicit controls.
  4. Use 100% to judge pores, eyes and fine hairs. Scroll to zoom around the pointer; choose Hand or hold Space and drag to move the image while a tool is selected. Panning follows small movements immediately at every zoom, including Fit and small images, without snapping back. Press 0 to fit.
  5. Open Layers or History from View. Toggle layer visibility, adjust opacity, and add a mask to reveal or hide parts of a correction.
  6. Choose Crop, press C / Ctrl+T, or use Image → Crop frame. Drag crop edges to trim, drag inside or grab the center + to move freely on X/Y at any size, or drag outside to draw a new selection. The frame can extend past image edges without changing size; checkerboard margins remain transparent in PNG. Arrows nudge one pixel; Shift+arrows move ten; Shift-drag locks movement to the initial X or Y axis until Shift is released. Choose Free, Original, 1:1, 4:5, 3:2 or 16:9. Handles trim or extend the crop canvas without rescaling the photo. The round handle and angle field rotate only the frame; the photo stays upright. Shift snaps 15° steps or preserves crop proportions. A rotated frame leaves transparent corners in PNG. Reset frame restores the hidden margins. Crop mode temporarily folds the preset and retouch panels to leave room around the image. Hold Space and drag to pan freely; hold a frame or handle drag at the canvas edge to keep scrolling. 0 / Fit shows both the frame and photo. Enter / Apply commits one undo step, and Esc / Cancel discards the preview. Exiting restores the panels. Saved workspaces, masks and exports retain the frame.
  7. The left gallery applies presets; the right rail switches retouch controls, and Tools / History / Layers share the inspector. Library shows your photos in a grid, and Edit returns to the canvas. Use View → Preset sidebar, Tab or focus mode to give the image more room.
  8. Save the workspace with Ctrl+S. Use Export for a finished photo. Choose Original size and PNG for lossless pixel inspection; JPEG is compressed. Export creates a separate versioned file.

Manual cleanup

  • Liquify: drag to push pixels. Start with a small brush and short movements.
  • Spot heal: click or drag over a small mark. It transfers nearby texture with color matching.
  • Clone stamp: Alt+click clean texture, then paint the destination. Choose source also works. Enable alignment to keep the source offset between strokes.
  • Patch: draw a closed selection around the repair area, then drag it onto clean texture and confirm the repair.
  • Heal and Clone can sample other visible layers. Paint on an unlocked, visible layer with nonzero opacity; a black mask hides the correction.

Ctrl+Z undoes a gesture; Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes it. Hold \ to inspect the original. Ctrl+, opens Preferences; Ctrl+K finds commands. Preference controls include UI size, zoom speed, brush defaults, autosave and export quality.

Portrait and batch controls

  • Light + color: Whites/Blacks control the tonal endpoints. Dodge and Burn paint local brightness; set the EV amount, brush strength and softness. Erase removes painted coverage. Hold Space to pan.
  • Clothing wrinkles: paint narrow crease shading. The brush keeps fine weave and broad drape; avoid seams and printed patterns. It is a manual correction, so inspect its result on complex garments.
  • Stray hair: trace isolated strands with a small brush. The tool protects broad edges and isolated spots, but you should keep the brush clear of eyebrows and lashes.
  • Skin: Keep skin detail controls fine texture during smoothing. Detect face skin, choose a source face as the skin-color reference, then use Unify selected skin tones. Skin lightness stays separate from the color match; refine the mask for neck and body coverage.
  • Brush size: −/+ and brackets change size. For Patch, −/+ changes feather width. Saved strokes retain their own sizes.
  • Batch: select photos, choose Portrait / Light + color / Background, then Sync. Local strokes and crop geometry stay with each photo. Adapt exposure compensates up to 1.5 EV of source-brightness differences. Auto Retouch selected keeps existing lighting and color. Preparation has progress and pause/resume controls.
  • Face focus: Analyze selected faces reports relative face and eye detail, and Rate sharpest faces marks up to five picks. Compare within a shoot and inspect expressions yourself. Scores are recomputed after reopening a workspace.

AI and hardware

Auto checks GPU results against CPU before accepting DirectML. CPU remains available when GPU qualification fails. The beta has been checked locally on an Intel UHD GPU; other GPUs need tester verification. The experimental Burn CPU provider is available but can be substantially slower. CUDA and CoreML are not bundled for this Windows beta.

Use 64-bit Windows 10 version 2004 or later, or Windows 11, with a current graphics driver. The development checks used 16 GB RAM. Smaller-memory systems and other hardware are not yet qualified. High-resolution editing and several active AI stages can consume several GB of memory.

Saved data and privacy

Preferences, recovery workspaces and AI caches are kept under %LOCALAPPDATA%\HasturRetouch. Your saved session refers to the imported files; keep those files available. Uninstalling removes program files and leaves your photos, exported images and workspace data in place. Recovery opens the latest usable workspace automatically.

No photo uploads, telemetry, account login or automatic feedback sending are included. Clicking an email link opens your mail app; you decide what to send. Models, licenses and source notices are included in the installation. See third-party notices and Rust dependency notices.
